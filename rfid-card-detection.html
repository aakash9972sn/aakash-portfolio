RFID Card
Detection System.

A contactless identification system in which an Arduino UNO talks to an MFRC522 reader over SPI, reads the Unique Identifier (UID) of a nearby card, checks it against a registered list, and responds with a green or red LED, a short beep and a line on the Serial Monitor.

ControllerArduino UNO R3 (ATmega328P, 16 MHz)
ReaderMFRC522 · ISO/IEC 14443 A
InterfaceSPI (D10–D13)
FeedbackGreen / red LED, active buzzer, serial
LanguageArduino C++

01 / THEORY

How passive RFID works.

The reader drives a 13.56 MHz alternating magnetic field through its antenna coil. When a passive card enters the field, a voltage is induced in the card's coil, which powers its chip. There's no battery in the card.

e = −N · dΦB/dt   →   for ΦB = Φm sin ωt,   e = −NωΦm cos ωt

The reader sends commands by modulating its carrier. The card replies through load modulation: it changes the loading on its antenna, and the reader detects that change. The free-space wavelength at 13.56 MHz is about 22 m, so at a few centimetres the link is pure near-field magnetic coupling, not radio broadcasting. The reader antenna is a resonant LC circuit tuned near f0 = 1 / (2π√LC).

Electrical precaution: the UNO uses 5 V logic, but the MFRC522 is a 3.3 V device. The reader is powered from 3.3 V, and the SCK, MOSI, SS and RST lines pass through level conversion.

04 / FIRMWARE

Detection loop.

  1. InitializeSerial, SPI bus, reader and output pins
  2. PollWait for a new card in the field
  3. Read UIDAnticollision, selection and UID transfer
  4. CompareMatch full UID and its length
  5. RespondLED, beep and serial log, then halt the card
#include <SPI.h>
#include <MFRC522.h>

#define SS_PIN 10
#define RST_PIN 9
#define GREEN_LED 6
#define RED_LED 7
#define BUZZER 8

MFRC522 rfid(SS_PIN, RST_PIN);
const byte registeredUID[] = {0x12, 0x34, 0x56, 0x78}; // replace with your card's UID

bool isRegisteredCard() {
  if (rfid.uid.size != sizeof(registeredUID)) return false;
  for (byte i = 0; i < sizeof(registeredUID); i++)
    if (rfid.uid.uidByte[i] != registeredUID[i]) return false;
  return true;
}

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(9600);
  SPI.begin();
  rfid.PCD_Init();
  pinMode(GREEN_LED, OUTPUT); pinMode(RED_LED, OUTPUT); pinMode(BUZZER, OUTPUT);
  Serial.println("Place an RFID card near the reader");
}

void loop() {
  if (!rfid.PICC_IsNewCardPresent() || !rfid.PICC_ReadCardSerial()) return;
  Serial.print("UID:");
  for (byte i = 0; i < rfid.uid.size; i++) {
    Serial.print(rfid.uid.uidByte[i] < 0x10 ? " 0" : " ");
    Serial.print(rfid.uid.uidByte[i], HEX);
  }
  bool ok = isRegisteredCard();
  Serial.println(ok ? "  REGISTERED" : "  UNREGISTERED");
  digitalWrite(ok ? GREEN_LED : RED_LED, HIGH);
  digitalWrite(BUZZER, HIGH); delay(150); digitalWrite(BUZZER, LOW);
  delay(850);
  digitalWrite(GREEN_LED, LOW); digitalWrite(RED_LED, LOW);
  rfid.PICC_HaltA();
  rfid.PCD_StopCrypto1();
}

The comparison checks the UID length as well as its bytes, because cards can carry 4-, 7- or 10-byte UIDs. To register a card, I scan it, copy its UID from the Serial Monitor into registeredUID and upload the sketch again.

06 / LIMITATIONS

Known limitations.

UID ≠ security

Some UIDs can be copied or changed. A UID match is identification, not authentication.

Short range

Designed for close-proximity reads. Orientation, metal and supply noise all affect detection.

HF only

Cannot read 125 kHz LF or UHF tags.

No memory of time

Without an RTC or storage there are no timestamps and no history of scans.
