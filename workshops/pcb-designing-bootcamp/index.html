01 / PROGRAMME OVERVIEW

About the Workshop.

Connecting theoretical circuit principles with practical considerations for reliable electronic hardware.

The PCB Designing Bootcamp, conducted on 13–14 February 2026 by the AICTE IDEA Lab at Delhi Technological University, was a technical training programme focused on understanding the process of designing and developing Printed Circuit Boards.

Printed Circuit Boards form the physical foundation of most modern electronic systems. They provide mechanical support for electronic components and establish electrical connections through conductive copper traces.

The workshop introduced the relationship between electronic circuit design and practical PCB development. An important part of the learning experience was working with EDWinXP, an Electronic Design Automation (EDA) software package used for schematic capture, PCB layout development and manufacturing documentation.

The experience helped connect concepts learned in electronics coursework with the practical considerations involved in developing reliable electronic hardware.

⚡ Three Main Stages

The bootcamp was structured around three core pillars of hardware development:

1. Fabrication

Translating digital layouts into physical bare boards using FR-4 laminates and copper etching.

2. Assembly

Mounting and soldering through-hole and surface-mount components according to package specifications.

3. Quality Assurance

Multi-tiered verification, optical inspection, continuity checks, and functional hardware validation.

02 / HARDWARE ARCHITECTURE

Technical Fundamentals.

Understanding the physical architecture, material composition, and functional elements of Printed Circuit Boards.

What Is a Printed Circuit Board?

A Printed Circuit Board (PCB) is a structure used to mechanically support electronic components and electrically connect them using conductive pathways, commonly made of copper.

Unlike traditional point-to-point wiring, which is fragile and difficult to reproduce, PCB technology allows complex electronic circuits to be built in a compact, organized, and industrially scalable manner.

Core Application Domains:

Embedded systems & microcontrollers Communication equipment & RF Consumer electronics Industrial automation Robotics & control systems Power electronics IoT devices & sensors Medical instrumentation

PCB Layer Structures

Depending on circuit complexity and high-frequency shielding requirements, PCBs are produced in different layer configurations:

Single-Layer PCB:

Conductive copper on only one side of the substrate. Suitable for simple, low-density circuits.

Double-Layer PCB:

Copper traces on both top and bottom sides. Plated through-hole vias establish layer-to-layer connections.

Multilayer PCB (Conceptual Context):

Multiple copper layers separated by insulating dielectric prepreg, with dedicated power and ground planes.

* Note: Multilayer board technology was covered as conceptual engineering context during the bootcamp; no complex multilayer board was personally fabricated.

Main Elements of a PCB

Substrate (FR-4)

The insulating structural base material providing mechanical rigidity. Woven fiberglass cloth reinforced with flame-retardant epoxy resin.

Copper Traces

Precision-etched conductive pathways that carry electrical signals between components, replacing individual wiring harnesses.

Pads

Exposed copper areas designed specifically for soldering component pins (either surface-mount pads or through-hole annular rings).

Vias

Drilled holes with electroplated conductive copper barrels that interconnect traces across different physical board layers.

Solder Mask

Protective polymer coating that insulates copper tracks, prevents environmental oxidation, and stops molten solder from bridging adjacent pins.

Silkscreen Legend

Printed epoxy markings detailing component reference designators (R1, C1, U1), pin 1 indicators, and polarity markings for assembly.

Component Footprints

The exact physical layout pattern of pads, holes, and mechanical courtyard clearance boundaries needed to mount an electronic part.

03 / EDA SOFTWARE SUITE

Designing with EDWinXP.

EDWinXP (Electronic Design for Windows) is an integrated Electronic Design Automation software package developed for electronic circuit schematic capture, PCB layout development, and manufacturing documentation.

MODULE A / LOGICAL DOMAIN

Schematic Editor

The Schematic Editor represents electronic circuits using standardized engineering symbols and electrical net connectivity.

  • Selecting components from the comprehensive EDWinXP part library
  • Placing schematic symbols and structuring logical circuit hierarchy
  • Establishing electrical connections using wires, buses, and named nets
  • Assigning suitable physical component packages (THT or SMT)
  • Adding component reference designators and parameter values
  • Compiling electrical netlists for forward-annotation to layout
MODULE B / PHYSICAL DOMAIN

PCB Layout Editor

The PCB Layout Editor translates the logical netlist into an exact, manufacturable physical board geometry.

  • Defining the physical PCB outline, mounting holes, and keep-out areas
  • Placing component footprints according to signal flow and thermal criteria
  • Adjusting part positions, rotation angles, and lead orientations
  • Selecting relevant board layers (Top Copper, Bottom Copper, Silk, Mask)
  • Maintaining required electrical clearance between adjacent conductive objects
MODULE C / ROUTING ENGINE

Routing Tools

EDWinXP provides tools for establishing physical conductive pathways between component pads.

  • Manual Routing: Full designer control over trace paths, angles, and layer transitions.
  • Semi-Automatic Routing: Assists point-to-point path finding while adhering to active clearance rules.
  • Automatic Routing: Uses algorithmic solvers to route complex nets based on pre-defined constraint sets.
Engineering Judgment Required: While auto-routers can calculate geometric paths, human engineering judgment remains essential to manage critical return paths, noise coupling, and high-current trace widths.
MODULE D / VERIFICATION ENGINE

Design Rule Checking (DRC)

DRC audits the PCB layout against specified physical and mechanical fabrication constraints.

  • Checking trace-to-trace clearance to prevent electrical bridging
  • Verifying pad-to-trace spacing and annular ring integrity
  • Detecting unrouted airwires, dangling stubs, or shorted nets
  • Validating drill hole margins relative to board outlines and copper planes
DRC Scope: DRC verifies defined geometric layout rules; it cannot verify whether the circuit is functionally correct in its theoretical electrical behavior.
MODULE E / MANUFACTURING OUTPUTS

Fabrication Manager

The Fabrication Manager produces the industry-standard manufacturing documentation needed to construct the bare board.

  • Generates standard RS-274X Extended Gerber artwork files for every layer
  • Outputs NC Drill (Excellon) files with exact coordinate drilling data
  • Produces drill charts detailing hole diameters, tolerances, and quantities
  • Creates fabrication drawings and board outline specifications
MODULE F / ADVANCED EDA CAPABILITIES

Simulation & Visualization

Beyond schematic capture and layout, EDWinXP includes integrated circuit simulation (SPICE-based analysis) and 3D board visualization tools.

  • Mixed-mode circuit simulation for analog and digital waveforms
  • Electromagnetic field analysis and thermal estimation
  • 3D board visualization for mechanical enclosure collision checks
Workshop Scope Boundary: These capabilities represent features built into EDWinXP; they were introduced conceptually during the bootcamp rather than personally executed for complex simulated designs.

06 / ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES

Key Technical Concepts Learned.

Essential principles bridging theoretical circuit schematics with physical board manufacturability.

CONCEPT 01

Schematic vs. PCB Layout

A schematic describes what is electrically connected using standardized logical symbols. A PCB layout describes where physical component footprints and conductive copper tracks are placed in three-dimensional space.

CONCEPT 02

Electrical Nets & Netlists

A net represents a logical group of component pins intended to be connected at the same electrical potential. PCB trace routing physically implements these nets with copper tracks.

CONCEPT 03

Component Footprints

Selecting an accurate footprint matching the physical lead pitch and package dimensions is vital. A mismatch between physical part dimensions and pad geometries prevents successful soldering and assembly.

CONCEPT 04

Trace Routing & Widths

Trace routing directly governs connectivity, available board area, manufacturability, and signal integrity. Trace widths must be appropriately sized to handle target currents without excessive resistive heating.

CONCEPT 05

PCB Layers & Stackup

Copper foil layers provide electrical current paths, while solder mask protects copper from oxidation and solder bridging, and silkscreen ink identifies components and test points for assembly technicians.

CONCEPT 06

Design Rule Checking (DRC)

Automated DRC verifies that copper clearances, trace widths, annular rings, and drill margins satisfy the physical constraints of fabrication before boards are sent to manufacturing.

CONCEPT 07

Design for Manufacturing (DFM)

A successful electronic board design must not only satisfy its electrical schematic requirements, but must also be practical and economical to fabricate, assemble, and inspect without defects.

08 / TAKEAWAYS & PERSPECTIVE

Engineering Insights.

Four foundational principles that govern reliable electronic hardware development.

01 / LOGIC VS GEOMETRY

Electrical correctness is only the beginning.

An electrically correct schematic still needs a physically valid PCB layout. Spatial clearances, copper thickness, thermal dissipation, and parasitic inductances all govern whether a physical board functions reliably.

02 / FLOORPLANNING

Component placement matters decisively.

Placement dictates routing complexity, board size, thermal distribution, signal integrity, connector accessibility, and ease of automated assembly. Good placement makes routing straightforward; poor placement makes routing impossible.

03 / ERROR PREVENTION

Verification is essential before fabrication.

Design Rule Checking and netlist connectivity checks help detect spacing violations and open circuits while still in software, preventing costly scrap and rework during physical manufacturing runs.

04 / LIFECYCLE THINKING

Hardware development is a connected process.

Schematic capture, PCB layout, fabrication constraints, component assembly, and quality assurance inspection must be considered concurrently rather than treated as isolated, disconnected steps.
